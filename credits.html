02 / Research

Amazon Echo and MediaTek research

The wider LibreEcho development work draws on publicly available reverse-engineering and device-enablement work. These projects are acknowledged as upstream research and tooling; LibreEcho does not claim their work as its own.

  • Amonet — MediaTek BootROM exploit research and exploit-chain foundations.
  • R0rt1z2/amonet — Amazon and MediaTek BootROM/LK exploit-chain work.
  • k4y0z — Amazon/MediaTek exploit, bootloader and device research.
  • mtkclient — MediaTek communication, flashing and payload infrastructure. Related LibreEcho development notes identify generic_stage1.c as adapted from mtkclient and credit B. Kerler and k4y0z.

The Echo Gen 2 / MT8163 work also includes an independent amonet-k32 development repository. Any source, payload, binary or tool distributed from it must retain its own component-level copyright and licence notices.

Linux and embedded support code

Some low-level support components retain separate notices, including code copyrighted by Jörg Mische and components distributed under GPL and BSD-style licences. Those notices must remain with the applicable source or binary distribution.

04 / Audio

Shairport Sync

LibreEcho currently uses Shairport Sync for AirPlay audio support.

  • Project author and maintainer: Mike Brady.
  • When distributing Shairport Sync, retain its upstream COPYING file and complete LICENSES/ directory.
  • Record the exact source revision used by LibreEcho with any binary distribution.

Shairport Sync acknowledges James Wah (abrasive), James Laird and other Shairport contributors. Its AirPlay 2 documentation also acknowledges JD Smith, ejurgensen, ckdo, invano and Charles Omer, and notes that much of its AirPlay 2 functionality is based on ideas developed in openairplay/airplay2-receiver.

Those acknowledgements belong to the upstream project; LibreEcho does not claim their work as its own.
